Our research

From traits to the globe: three scales of understanding life's macroscopic patterns

Our group works across three scales — plant functional traits, biodiversity, and biomes. We ask how traits differentiate across biomes, how climate change drives the re-delineation of biome boundaries, and whether more fundamental theory can characterize diversity patterns.

Our research

Three core research directions

01

Macroecology of plant functional traits

How plant functional traits vary across species, communities, and environments — and the biogeographic relationships between traits and ecosystem functioning.

02

Biome dynamics under climate change

Refining biome classifications with multi-source evidence, identifying areas sensitive to climate change, and forecasting shifts in ecosystem functioning.

03

Foundational macroecological theory

Building foundational theory for macroecology around the mechanisms shaping diversity patterns, community assembly, and the distribution of biodiversity.

Methods & tools

Treating the Earth as a living laboratory

Data

Global data integration

Integrating forest inventories, vegetation plots, satellite remote sensing (Landsat, MODIS, Sentinel), and global environmental gridded data into multi-source standardized datasets.

Methods

Statistical modeling & machine learning

Applying hierarchical Bayesian models, generalized additive models, random forests, and interpretable deep learning, combined with causal inference and uncertainty quantification.

Collaboration

International research network

We maintain long-term collaborations with the Swiss Federal Institute for Forest, Snow and Landscape Research (WSL) and ETH Zurich, and participate in several global-scale data initiatives.
