School of Life Sciences, Nanjing University · Functional Macroecology Lab

Understanding how the form and function of plants shape the macroscopic patterns of life on Earth

We study the macroecological patterns of plant functional traits, biodiversity, and biomes — and how interactions among vegetation, climate, and global change shape these patterns.

Our research

Understanding ecosystems across scales

01

Macroecology of plant functional traits

How plant functional traits vary across species, communities, and environments — and the biogeographic relationships between traits and ecosystem functioning.

02

Biome dynamics under climate change

Refining biome classifications with multi-source evidence, identifying areas sensitive to climate change, and forecasting shifts in ecosystem functioning.

03

Foundational macroecological theory

Building foundational theory for macroecology around the mechanisms shaping diversity patterns, community assembly, and the distribution of biodiversity.

Why macroecology

From local observations to global patterns

Many ecological patterns only emerge once we connect regions and scales. We combine ecological theory with reproducible data analysis to understand vegetation–climate relationships and their responses to global change — supporting forest dynamics forecasting and carbon-cycle assessment.

About the PI

PI photo (to be replaced)
Haozhi Ma
Tenure-Track Associate Professor, School of Life Sciences, Nanjing University · National Youth Talent Program Awardee

Haozhi Ma is a tenure-track associate professor at the School of Life Sciences, Nanjing University, and a National Youth Talent Program awardee. His research focuses on the macroecology of plant functional traits and biomes. He has published 14 SCI papers, including first/corresponding-author papers in Nature Ecology & Evolution and Nature Plants, with work positively cited by papers in Nature and Science. He received the 2022 China National Award for Outstanding Self-Funded Students Abroad and is a core member of an SNSF Advanced Grant project.
